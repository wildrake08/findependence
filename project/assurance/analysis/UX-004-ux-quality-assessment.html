Findependence UX Quality Review

This assesses the whole browser experience of the current build against thirteen separate qualities, from whether the product is worth using to whether its tone fits a household's money. UX-001 and UX-002 covered journeys, and UX-003 covered visual and interaction craft; their fixes are in this build, so this review doesn't repeat them. It asks what still limits the experience in each dimension, keeps severe weaknesses visible instead of averaging them away, and recommends the smallest set of changes that would matter most.

Prepared by ACT-002, the same agent that built this interface, so read it with that bias in mind. No target user has used it. Every figure comes from the demo family or a seeded test household, whose names, figures, and relationships are invented. This is a proposal, not a canonical change.

1 Executive UX diagnosis

Principal user outcomes

  • See whether the household will run short before the next paycheck, and when (CAP-011).
  • Keep the record current: balances, debts, items (CAP-010).
  • Try a change before committing to it: a job loss, borrowing, a debt paid faster (CAP-007).
  • Judge retirement against one's own target (CAP-012).
  • Control who sees and owns what, and agree on shared things (OUT-004, OUT-005).
  • Leave with one's record, and bring it into another household (CAP-002, CAP-009).

Most important strengths

  • Honest, specific feedback. Every action reports what actually happened, in words (“Added “Bus pass”.”); a session lost before a save says “your last action was not saved”; a refused save says so where the page opens.
  • Nothing is judged or suggested. A glossary test scans every page for judgment words, retirement fills in nothing for the member, and plans never mix with real totals.
  • Privacy is the default and is legible. Every item says who owns it and who else can see it; shared changes wait for every owner.
  • Measured accessibility and precision. axe-core finds nothing on 64 page states at two widths, a Tab walk reaches every control in order with focus contrast of at least 13.39:1, and a geometry check finds no misalignment or overflow at 1200, 390, or 320 px (UI-RUN-003 and this review).
  • Fast and stable. Every page is complete on first paint, with no scripts, fonts, or late assets, and under 25 ms of server time at 200 items.

Most consequential deficiencies

  1. A double-sent form creates the record twice (Resilience). Sending the same “Add” form twice produced two items (40 to 42 in the demo). A jointly owned duplicate can't then be removed by one owner.
  2. Joint accounts show one member's part under account-level labels (Trustworthiness). Coming up on Dad's home marks October 1 “Below zero” at −$1,790.00 for joint checking, which holds $850.00; Mom's paycheck is private, so Dad's view leaves it out. A 67-word paragraph explains this; the column still says “Balance after”.
  3. A 15-minute idle lock discards the action in progress, with no way to adjust it (Accessibility, with a security tension). People who need more time, including screen-reader and switch users, lose what they typed.
  4. A plan is deleted at once, without confirmation (Agency), unlike items, and the message doesn't name it.
  5. Keeping the record current is slow (Efficiency). Dad's six accounts and debts each need their own page and form; amounts can't be changed at all (CP-015, deferred until the security review).

Systemic root causes

  • Actions aren't idempotent. Forms carry a session-wide CSRF token, not a per-form token, so the server can't tell a resend from a new request.
  • Views are computed per member; money moves per account. CP-014's attachments narrowed this, but a private paycheck into a joint account still leaves each owner with a partial view.
  • Security choices made for a shared device (one session, idle lock, no recovery, no backup) are sound for the alpha, but the interface itself doesn't state their consequences where they bite.
  • Write-once records (ASM-021) make correction and upkeep expensive until CP-015 is built.

Severe dimension-specific weaknesses

None is critical in the alpha with made-up data. Three would be serious in a pilot with real households: Resilience (duplicates, no backup), Trustworthiness (partial joint views), and Accessibility (timing without adjustment, and no screen-reader evidence yet).

Major cross-dimensional tensions

  • Security vs accessibility and resilience: the idle lock and single session protect a shared device but lose work.
  • Privacy vs information quality: private items make joint-account views partial.
  • Upkeep efficiency vs integrity: editing amounts needs re-keying that is waiting for the security review.
  • Compact home vs discoverability: five features are reached from one row of links.

2 Dimension-by-dimension assessment

Confidence reflects the evidence above; “expert judgment” means no user has confirmed it.

1 · Utility Moderate gaps confidence medium

Current state
Every principal outcome has a working path: cash flow at 14 days, 60 days, and 12 months; plans; goals; retirement; sharing and consent; leaving and bringing a record in.
Strengths
Capabilities are expressed as household questions (“Will we run short?”), not as system objects.
Deficiencies
Amounts can't change (CP-015 deferred). No backup of the household's record (CP-010 A: say so, accepted for the study). Membership is fixed at setup (ASM-022). No way to find an item at scale: home at 200 items is 16,360 px on phones (scale README).
Root causes
Security-review dependencies and study-scope decisions, each recorded.
Recommendations
None to build now; each gap has a recorded decision. P4 makes the backup and recovery limits visible in the app (section 6).

2 · Effectiveness Minor gaps confidence medium

Current state
Tasks complete through plain forms with a redirect after each post, so refresh doesn't resubmit. Errors keep what was typed and sit under the field.
Deficiencies
Duplicate creation on a resent form (assigned to Resilience, R-1). Correcting a wrong amount means delete and re-add, losing links and history; a joint item can't be deleted by one owner (CP-015).
Recommendations
P1 (section 6).

3 · Efficiency Moderate confidence high for counts, low for time

Current state
Adding an item is one form on home. Pages respond in well under 0.1 s.
Deficiencies
The weekly check-in: Dad's six accounts and debts need six page visits and six forms; debts now start from the last rate and minimum (UX-002 R2). UX-002 R3 (one page for all balances) was never decided.
Root causes
One page per record, which suits reading but not batch upkeep.
Recommendations
H1: decide UX-002 R3 (section 6), after timing the check-in in the walkthroughs.

4 · Learnability Moderate expert judgment; confidence low–medium

Current state
One word per concept, enforced by a glossary test; every card explains itself in a short hint.
Deficiencies
First use: an empty home opens with an empty Coming up, then asks for items, values, and accounts in that order. Coming up needs an account with a balance, but its empty text asks only for dates. The totals card shows a table of $0.00 before anything exists. Five features are reachable only from one link row inside Coming up.
Root causes
Empty states describe their own card, not the dependency between cards.
Recommendations
H2: actionable empty states (section 6). X4: header links (experiment).

5 · Agency Moderate confidence high

Current state
Nothing is chosen for the member; no defaults on how often or which direction matter. Irreversible item actions preview and confirm. Shared changes need every owner's agreement and can be withdrawn.
Deficiencies
A plan, with all its steps, is deleted on one press, with no confirmation and a message that doesn't name it. Amounts can't be revised (CP-015).
Recommendations
P3 (section 6).

6 · Trustworthiness High confidence medium

Current state
The app says what it counts, where data lives (“Everything stays on this device”), and that the alpha takes made-up data only. Estimates say “about” and give their assumptions.
Deficiencies
A partial view of a joint account uses account-level labels (“Balance after”, “Below zero”); the caveat is a 67-word paragraph above the table. Two consequential limits, that a forgotten passphrase can't be recovered and that there's no backup, appear nowhere in the app or the setup command (README and consent materials only; CP-010 A).
Root causes
Privacy-preserving computation with labels written for the complete case; limits documented outside the product.
Recommendations
P2 and P4 (section 6).

7 · Information Quality Minor confidence medium

Current state
Every amount shows its schedule; dates are written out; estimates are whole dollars and rounded to $100; numeric columns align.
Deficiencies
The caveats before each projection table are long (Coming up opens with a 67-word paragraph; the 60 days and 12 months open with similar ones), which competes with the table they explain. The partial-view labelling is assigned to Trustworthiness.
Recommendations
P2 shortens the Coming up caveat as a side effect; no separate change.

8 · Accessibility High (unverified) confidence medium for automated, low for practical

Current state
Automated: clean. Labels, error links, table roles on phones, reflow to 320 px, focus contrast, and no motion.
Deficiencies
Timing: the 15-minute idle lock has no warning (no JavaScript) and can't be adjusted; the action in progress is lost (WCAG 2.2.1 Timing Adjustable, unless the limit counts as essential for security). No screen-reader, voice-control, or magnification session has been run (WI-024 R10, deferred). Compact buttons are 32 px, which meets WCAG 2.5.8 but is below platform touch guidance (UX-003 X1).
Recommendations
P5 (a ChangeProposal). V1: the deferred assistive-technology walkthroughs before any pilot.

9 · Adaptability Minor for the study confidence medium

Current state
Phone layouts are designed, not compressed; keyboard and touch both work; no page scrolls sideways at 320 px.
Deficiencies
English and US dollars only, and Washington-specific notes, which fit STUDY-001's scope. Large households: no search or grouping at 200 items (a STUDY-001 question). One shared device (DEF-030).
Recommendations
None now; each is a recorded scope decision.

10 · Resilience High confidence high

Current state
Redirect after post (no resubmit on refresh); a second copy changing the file is refused with “Nothing was saved” and the latest shown (F-16, tested); a lost session says the action wasn't saved.
Deficiencies
Resent forms duplicate records (E3). An unknown address returns bare “Not found” with no page or way back. Work in progress is lost on lock (see Accessibility). Device loss loses everything (CP-010 A, accepted for the study).
Recommendations
P1; H3 (a real not-found page).

11 · Craftsmanship Strong confidence high

Current state
UX-003's twelve fixes are in: tokens for colour, focus, control height, type, and radii; aligned tables and forms; complete error, empty, danger, and focus states. geometry.py passes on every page at three widths.
Deficiencies
Chromium only; Safari may draw the white selects differently (UX-003 residual risk). The 404 page has no styling (counted under Resilience).
Recommendations
V2: one Safari and Firefox pass.

12 · Aesthetic Quality Adequate, restrained expert judgment

Current state
A single column of white cards, system type, one accent, semantic colour only for state. Hierarchy reads page title, then sections, then content.
Deficiencies
No observable compositional defect. Home is long and uniform: eight cards of equal weight, so the eye gets little help finding the one that matters today. This is a judgment, not a defect; UX-002's ordering (waiting first, then coming up) already addresses priority.
Recommendations
None. A distinct visual identity would be preference, not remedy.

13 · Experiential Appropriateness Appropriate expert judgment

Current state
Calm, plain, and non-judgmental, which fits household money and PRI-001. No urgency, celebration, or animation; destructive actions are red and confirmed; errors explain without blame.
Deficiencies
One tonal risk: a red “Below zero” on a partial view can alarm without cause (resolved by P2's labelling, not by softening the colour, which REQ-139 relies on).
Recommendations
None separately; test tone in the walkthroughs (V1).

3 Derived quality assessment

Seamlessness

The nominal path is close to seamless: a member reads home top to bottom, acts in a form, and lands back where they were with a sentence saying what happened. Friction comes from five specific causes, not a general quality:

  1. Resilience: a resent form duplicates (P1).
  2. Trustworthiness: a partial joint view looks complete (P2).
  3. Accessibility and resilience: the idle lock discards work (P5).
  4. Efficiency: upkeep is one page per account (H1), and amounts can't change (CP-015).
  5. Learnability: first use doesn't show the dependency between accounts, dates, and Coming up (H2).

Interface craftsmanship

Implementation and aesthetics are resolved after UX-003: every measured criterion passes, there is no motion to misfire, and performance leaves no perceptible wait. The remaining limits are evidence limits (one browser engine, no assistive-technology session) and one unstyled state (404), not design or system-rule gaps.

4 Journey findings

J1 · Add an item, and double-click Add High occasional · system-wide · confidence high

User outcome
One item recorded per intent.
Context
Any member; a double click, a slow page, or a resend after the back button. Tested on add item; the other creating forms (values, accounts, readings, plans, steps) carry the same session token, so the same is expected but not yet shown.
Evidence
E3: two posts of the same add-item form → items 40 to 42, both 303 with “Added”.
Primary dimension
Resilience. Secondary: Effectiveness (a joint duplicate can't be removed by one owner).
Root cause
Forms carry the session's CSRF token, which is valid for every request, so there's no per-form identity to deduplicate on.
Recommendation
P1.
Tradeoffs
A deliberate second “Add” of the same form (to record two identical items) would need the page to reload first. That's rare, and the message tells the member.
Acceptance
Two posts carrying the same form token create one record; the second returns to the same page with “That was already saved.”
Validation
An ExUnit test per creating action; a deliberate-break check.

J2 · Will joint checking go below zero? High common for couples · journey · confidence medium

User outcome
Know whether the joint account runs short, and understand when the view is partial.
Context
Co-owners of an account who keep some items private (in the demo, both paychecks).
Evidence
E2: Dad's Coming up: joint checking $850.00; October 1, “Balance after −$1,790.00, Below zero”. The explanation is a 67-word paragraph above the table (REQ-138's note from UX-002 R1a).
Primary dimension
Trustworthiness. Secondary: Experiential Appropriateness (alarm without cause).
Root cause
Labels written for the complete case are reused for the partial case.
Recommendation
P2.
Tradeoffs
Two header wordings to maintain; the “Below zero” marker is unchanged (REQ-139).
Acceptance
When any co-owner's items may be missing, the column reads “Your part after”, and one sentence names the co-owner. When the view is complete, it reads “Balance after” with no caveat.
Validation
Tests on the demo (Dad partial, a single-owner account complete); in the walkthrough, both parents asked whether joint checking goes below zero and why the views differ.

J3 · Delete a plan Moderate occasional · component · confidence high

User outcome
Remove a plan knowingly, or not at all.
Evidence
E3: one post deletes “If Dad's job stops” with its three steps and redirects home with “Deleted the plan.” Items and leaving use /confirm/:action first.
Primary dimension
Agency.
Root cause
The confirm route covers only delete and relinquish.
Recommendation
P3.
Tradeoffs
One more step to delete a plan; plans are deleted rarely.
Acceptance
Delete plan shows a confirmation naming the plan and its step count; confirming returns to Plans with “Deleted the plan “If Dad's job stops”.”; going back leaves it unchanged.
Validation
Web test; deliberate-break check.

J4 · Fill the retirement assumptions slowly High for affected users occasional · system-wide · confidence medium

User outcome
Finish a seven-field form at one's own pace.
Context
Members who need more time: screen-reader, switch, or cognitive-access users, or anyone who steps away to find a statement.
Evidence
E3: after the session ends, a post returns to the unlock page with “your last action was not saved”; typed values aren't kept. E5: 15 minutes, fixed.
Primary dimension
Accessibility. Secondary: Resilience.
Root cause
A fixed idle limit chosen for a shared device, with no JavaScript to warn and no safe place to keep typed values across a lock.
Recommendation
P5.
Tradeoffs
A longer limit leaves an unlocked session open longer on a shared device.
Acceptance
A decision recorded by the security review: either an adjustable limit or a documented essential-for-security exception.
Validation
Security review; assistive-technology walkthrough of the retirement form.

J5 · First use Moderate once per member · journey · expert judgment

User outcome
Get to a useful Coming up quickly.
Evidence
E2: the empty home shows Coming up (“Add the date a bill or paycheck happens”), items, values, “Add an account or debt” as an outline button in the fourth card, and a totals table of $0.00.
Primary dimension
Learnability.
Root cause
Each card's empty state describes itself, not the dependency between them.
Recommendation
H2.
Tradeoffs
None material; text only.
Acceptance
With no account, Coming up says it needs an account's balance and dated items, linking to both; the totals table is replaced by one line until an item exists.

Web test; first-attempt success in the walkthrough (first task: “see what's coming up this week”).

User outcome
Bring all balances up to date.
Evidence

Primary dimension
Efficiency.

One page per record; UX-002 R3 not yet decided.
Recommendation

5 Systemic findings

  • State model: no per-form identity (J1). One root cause behind any duplicate record.
  • Conceptual model: member-computed views of shared accounts (J2). CP-014 made complete views possible when members attach and share; the labels didn't follow.
  • Destructive-action policy: confirmation is chosen per route, not by rule (J3). Proposed rule: confirm any deletion of a record that holds other records or history (items, plans, values); don't confirm removing a single plan step or a link, which can be re-added in one action.
  • Content: limits that matter at the worst moment (no recovery, no backup) live in documents, not in the product (P4).
  • Security design: timing and single-session choices shape accessibility and resilience (J4). These are for the security review, not the interface alone.

6 Recommendation portfolio

Ordered by expected improvement relative to cost, risk, and confidence.

Necessary corrections

P1 · One-time form tokens J1 system-wide

Problem
Resent forms create duplicate records.
Dimensions
Resilience; Effectiveness.
Implementation intent
Each creating form carries a random hidden token. The session remembers recently used tokens (a small bounded set). A post whose token was already used makes no change and redirects to where the first one went, with “That was already saved.” No JavaScript.
Dependencies
None; the session store already holds per-session state.
Tradeoffs · mitigation
Adding two identical items needs a page reload; the message says so.
Magnitude · reversibility · confidence
Small–medium · easy · high.
Acceptance
For every creating action, two posts with the same token create one record; a fresh page's token works.
Validation
ExUnit per action; mutation check (token ignored).

P2 · Label partial joint views as partial J2 journey

Problem
A member's part of a joint account reads as the account's balance.
Dimensions
Trustworthiness; Experiential Appropriateness.
Implementation intent
In Coming up, the 60 days, and the 12 months, when a co-owner's items may be missing, the balance column header becomes “Your part after” (or “Your part at the end”), and the caveat becomes one sentence: “Mom also owns Joint checking; Mom's items count only when shared with you and attached.” “Below zero” is unchanged (REQ-139).
Dependencies
REQ-138's note wording (UX-002 R1a) is interface wording within CP-002's delegation.
Tradeoffs · mitigation
Two header wordings; covered by tests.
Magnitude · reversibility · confidence
Small · easy · medium (needs the walkthrough).
Acceptance
As J2.
Validation
As J2.

P3 · Confirm and name plan deletion J3 component

Implementation intent
Route “Delete plan” through /confirm/delete_plan with the plan's name and step count; on success return to /plans with a message naming it. Adopt the confirmation rule in section 5.
Dimensions
Agency.
Magnitude · reversibility · confidence
Small · easy · high.
Acceptance · validation
As J3.

P4 · Say the unrecoverable limits where they matter Trust local

Problem
No passphrase recovery and no backup are stated only in the README and consent materials.
Implementation intent
The unlock page and mix findependence.setup each say: “A forgotten passphrase can't be recovered. There is no backup: if this device is lost, what's here is gone. Each person can save their own record from Leaving.” Consistent with CP-010 A (“say so”).
Dimensions
Trustworthiness; Resilience (informed precaution).
Tradeoffs
More text on the unlock page; one short paragraph.
Magnitude · reversibility · confidence
Small · easy · high.
Acceptance
Both statements on the unlock page and printed by setup; the glossary test passes.
Validation
Web and task tests.

P5 · Decide the idle-lock timing with the security review J4 architectural

Implementation intent
A ChangeProposal for the security review (DEF-026) with options: (A) a household-chosen idle limit at setup (15, 30, or 60 minutes); (B) keep 15 minutes and record it as essential for a shared device; (C) keep the discarded form encrypted to the member and restore it only to that member after they unlock (WCAG 2.2.5). Nothing is built before the review.
Dimensions
Accessibility; Resilience.
Tradeoffs
Security exposure (A, C) against lost work (B).
Magnitude · reversibility · confidence
Proposal now · — · medium.
Acceptance
The decision is recorded, and the walkthrough includes a slow completion of the retirement form.

High-value improvements

H1 · Decide UX-002 R3 (all balances on one page) J6

Time the weekly check-in in the WI-024 walkthroughs first, then decide. If built: one form listing the member's own accounts and debts, each row optional, errors per row with the others kept. Medium · easy to reverse · confidence low until timed.

H2 · Empty states that show the dependency J5

With no account, Coming up says: “Coming up needs an account's balance and bills or paychecks with dates,” linking to “Add an account” and to the item form. Until an item exists, the totals card says “Totals appear when you add items” instead of a $0.00 table. Small · easy · expert judgment.

H3 · A real not-found page

Unknown addresses render the normal page shell with “That page doesn't exist” and a link home, keeping the 404 status. Small · easy · high. Acceptance: a test that /nowhere returns 404 with the header and a home link.

Experiments

  • X4 · Header links. “Coming up · Plans · Retirement” in the header on every page, against the current single link row. Measure whether walkthrough participants find Plans and Retirement unaided, and the phone-height cost.
  • UX-003 X1–X3 (touch size, return to the acted-on card, one solid button per card) stay experiments for the same walkthroughs.

Verification needed before a pilot

  • V1 The WI-024 walkthroughs with a screen reader (NVDA with Firefox, VoiceOver with Safari), keyboard only, and 200% zoom, including the joint-account question (J2) and a slow retirement form (J4).
  • V2 One Safari and Firefox pass over add item, plan, unlock, and a debt's page.

8 Target UX state

  • Useful outcomes: as today; each gap left (editing amounts, backup, membership) is a recorded decision, and the app states the ones that can cost a member their data.
  • Correctly: sending a form twice records once. A joint account's view says whether it is the account or the member's part.
  • Effort removed: no duplicate cleanup; first use points straight to the account and dated items that make Coming up work; batch upkeep if H1 is adopted.
  • Understanding: empty states name what each view needs; labels match what is counted.
  • Control: every deletion of a record holding other records is previewed, named, and confirmed.
  • Trust: what is counted, what is private, and what can't be recovered are each stated where the member meets them.
  • Information: one sentence of caveat instead of a paragraph where the view is partial; none where it's complete.
  • Accessibility: automated checks stay clean, and the first assistive-technology evidence exists. The idle-lock decision is recorded, not implicit.
  • Adaptation: unchanged; one language, one currency, one device, by study scope.
  • Under failure: resends, unknown addresses, lost sessions, and outside file changes each end on a page that says what happened and offers the next step.
  • Craft, aesthetics, tone: unchanged from UX-003: aligned, restrained, calm, with no motion.
  • Complexity kept on purpose: consent for joint changes, private-by-default items, and write-once records until the security review.
