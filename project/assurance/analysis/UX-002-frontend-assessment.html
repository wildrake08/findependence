UX-002 · Frontend assessment · v0.5.0-alpha · 27 September 2026

Findependence Alpha UX Review

This assesses the browser interface as released in v0.5.0-alpha: the eight pages UX-001 covered, plus everything the alpha roadmap added (balances and debts, dated cash flow, the next 12 months, plans, goals, retirement, and bringing a record in). It follows the fixes UX-001 asked for, so it doesn't repeat them. It asks what still stands between a household member and each outcome, and recommends the smallest set of changes that would remove it.

Prepared by ACT-002, the same agent that built these versions, so a reviewer should read it with that bias in mind. No target user has used the interface. Every figure comes from the demo family, whose names, figures, and relationships are invented.

1 UX diagnosis

Principal user outcomes

  • Know whether the household will run short before the next paycheck, and when (the family's first problem in ROADMAP-ALPHA; CAP-011).
  • Keep the record current with little effort: balances, debts, and amounts that change (the roadmap's “five-minute check”; CAP-010).
  • See what a change would do before committing to it: a job loss, a side business, borrowing, a debt paid faster (CAP-007).
  • Judge retirement readiness against the family's own target, under assumptions they set (CAP-012).
  • Control who sees and owns what, and agree on shared things (UX-001's journeys, still central).
  • Leave with one's record, and bring it into a new household (CAP-002, CAP-009).

Most consequential friction

  1. Two members looking at the same joint account are shown two different futures. In the demo, joint checking holds $850.00. On Thursday, October 1, Dad's Coming up shows the balance after as −$1,790.00, marked “Below zero”, while Mom's shows $280.00. Each view counts only items that member owns, and the app has no idea which account an item is paid into or out of. The feature meant to answer “will we run short?” gives each parent a different, partial answer about money they share.
  2. Keeping the record current takes repeated entry and navigation. Each account or debt is updated on its own page. A debt's form asks for the rate and the minimum payment again every time, with empty fields, although neither usually changes.
  3. An item's amount can't be changed. When rent goes up, the only route is to delete the item and add it again, losing its links, marks, and history, and leaving plan steps that named it pointing at nothing. A joint item can't be deleted by one owner at all.

Systemic patterns

  • Computation follows members; money follows accounts. Coming up, the next 60 days, the next 12 months, and plans all compute from the viewing member's own items. That is right for privacy, but it doesn't match how a couple's joint money moves. It lies behind friction 1 and part of the plan findings.
  • Write-once records without a “what changed” path. Items are immutable (ASM-021). Balances got an append-only path (CP-013 A), but amounts didn't, and balance entry doesn't carry values forward. This lies behind frictions 2 and 3.
  • Tables stack into tall cards on phones. Each table row becomes 3–4 lines at 390 px. Dad's plan page is 4,312 px and Dad's home page 5,292 px on a phone. Home at 50 items is 7,671 px against a 7,812 px budget, so about 140 px remain for anything else home ever needs.
  • Features reached from a single row of links. The next 12 months, Plans, Goals, and Retirement are all links inside the Coming up card, and bringing a record in is a sentence in the Leaving card. There is no other route to them.

Strengths to preserve

  • Results say what happened (“Brought in 5 entries from your file. They're yours alone…”), and refusals say what and where, in words.
  • Errors sit at the field with what was typed kept, and a refused save says so where the page opens.
  • Nothing is scored or suggested. A judgment-word check runs over every page; retirement fills in nothing for the member.
  • Plans are kept apart from what's real, and every plan shows its effect beside things as they are.
  • Consequential actions preview first (bringing a record in), and only irreversible ones ask for confirmation.
  • Accessible foundations, checked every release: axe-core finds nothing on 53 page types at two widths, the Tab key reaches every control in order with visible focus, and no page scrolls sideways at 390 px.
  • Fast: every page responds in under 22 ms of server time at 200 items.

2 Journey findings

J1 · Will we run short before payday? Critical common · system-wide · confidence high

User outcome
Either parent can see, for the joint account, the days it would go below zero and by how much.
Shortest path
Open home; read Coming up for the joint account. One glance, the same answer for both parents.
Evidence
E4. Joint checking $850.00 as of Saturday, September 26. Dad's Coming up: October 1, −$1,790.00, “Below zero”, then −$1,835.00 and −$1,985.00. Mom's: October 1, $280.00; October 6, −$80.00. Mom's paycheck (September 29, +$1,980.00) is private to Mom, so Dad's view omits it; Dad's items are missing from Mom's. The page does say “Counts only items you own…”. E1: REQ-138 counts only items the member owns, and no item records which account it goes through.
Friction / failure
Outcome failure and loss of trust: both views are partial, they disagree about the same account on the same day, and a “Below zero” badge can be a false alarm or a missed one. The disclaimer asks the member to reconstruct the true balance in their head, which is exactly the task the feature exists to do. Sharing a paycheck doesn't help, because shared items aren't counted either.
Root cause
Architectural. Flows are attributed to members rather than to the account they move through. REQ-138 was narrowed to owned items for a sound reason: Grandma pays the tuition the kids share with their parents, which isn't the parents' money. That also shut out a spouse's paycheck that really does go into the joint account.
Recommendation
R1. Let an item say which account it's paid into or out of (optional; set by an owner). Compute an account's running balance from every item attached to it that the viewer can see, whether owned or shared, and say plainly when other owners of the account may have items the viewer can't see. Members who want one shared picture share their paycheck and attach it; privacy stays the default. Until R1 is decided, R1a: on a joint account, name what's missing (“Mom also owns Joint checking. Mom's private items aren't counted, so this is your part of the picture, not the account's balance.”).
Expected effect
When both parents attach and share their paychecks, both see the same balance after each day for joint checking. Without that, each is told precisely why their view is partial.
Acceptance criterion
With both paychecks shared and attached in the demo, Dad's and Mom's balance after each day for joint checking are identical for all 60 days (automated). With nothing attached, the page names the co-owner whose private items aren't counted (automated). In a walkthrough, both parents answer “Will joint checking go below zero before October 6, and by how much?” identically and correctly (to be baselined).
Validation
Core tests on the demo; the question above asked of each parent in the first walkthrough, before and after the change.

J2 · Keep balances current High pervasive (weekly or more) · journey · confidence high

User outcome
In a few minutes, update every account and debt so everything downstream is right.
Shortest path
One page listing each account and debt the member owns; type each new balance; save once.
Evidence
E1/E2: each account or debt has its own page; after saving, the member is on that page and must go back home for the next. Dad owns or co-owns five (joint checking, savings, and the HELOC with Mom; a Visa and a 401(k) alone), so that's 5 page visits and 5 saves. On a debt, the rate and minimum-payment fields render empty (value="" on Dad's Visa page) and are required, so 3 fields are typed where 1 changed.
Friction / failure
Repeated navigation and repeated entry, with a risk of mistyping a rate that didn't change. The “five-minute check” is at best several minutes, and more likely skipped, which makes every projection stale.
Root cause
Local and journey-level. The reading form doesn't carry forward the latest values, and readings were designed per item page with no batch view.
Recommendation
R2 (small): prefill a debt's rate and minimum payment from its latest balance. R3 (medium): an “Update balances” page, linked from Balances on home, with one row per account or debt the member owns: the latest balance and its date for reference, a new-balance field, and prefilled rate and minimum for debts. Save once; rows left empty are skipped; errors appear at their row with everything typed kept.
Expected effect
Updating five balances becomes one page and one save, typing only what changed.
Acceptance criterion
Updating Dad's five balances takes 1 page load and 1 submit (automated count). A debt's rate and minimum are prefilled (automated). Time to update them in a walkthrough is measured against today's flow (baseline first).
Validation
Interaction count scripted in tests; timed task in the walkthrough.

J3 · Correct an amount that changed High occasional · system-wide · confidence high

User outcome
When rent, pay, or a bill changes, record the new amount and keep everything tied to it.
Shortest path
Open the item; enter the new amount and from when; save.
Evidence
E1/E2: an item page offers sharing, owners, links, marks, “Give away” and “Delete”, and nothing to change the amount. E6: items are write-once (ASM-021); editing was deferred until after the security review (CP-008 A). Deleting removes its links and marks, and plan steps that named it then read “an item no longer there” (Plans.purge_item/2). Delete needs a sole owner, so the joint mortgage can't be deleted by either parent alone.
Friction / failure
The record drifts out of date, or the member rebuilds the item and loses what was attached to it: links, marks, and the plan steps that named it. For joint items there's no path at all without the other owner.
Root cause
Architectural: no append-only path for an item's amount, although CP-013 A built exactly that for balances.
Recommendation
R4. Decide (a ChangeProposal to ACT-001, with the security reviewer) whether an item's amount and schedule change as dated, append-only entries sealed like balance readings. Any owner of a joint item can add one without the other's agreement, as with balances. Everything computed uses the entry in force on each date. Until then, the item page should say how to change an amount today and what is lost.
Expected effect
Amounts stay current without rebuilding items; history shows each change and who made it.
Acceptance criterion
After changing rent from a future month, projections use the old amount before that month and the new one after; links, marks, and plan steps are unchanged; history lists the change (automated).
Validation
Core tests; the security review of the new sealed-entry path; a walkthrough task (“Your rent goes up next month”).

J4 · What if the job stops? Moderate occasional · local · confidence medium

User outcome
See whether and when the plan runs out of cash, and how bad it gets.
Shortest path
Open the plan; read one sentence: when cash first goes below zero, and its lowest point, with and without the plan.
Evidence
E2/E3: Dad's plan page is a 12-row table (without, with, difference); on a phone it's 4,312 px, each month 3–4 lines. The first below-zero month and the lowest point aren't stated; the reader scans for “Below zero” badges. It inherits J1: Mom's income isn't in Dad's plan.
Friction / failure
Comparison and memory burden: the answer is in the table but has to be assembled. On a phone, most of it is below the fold.
Root cause
Local: table-only presentation. Retirement already solved the same problem by summarizing and folding its table.
Recommendation
R5. Above the table, state the first month cash goes below zero and the lowest cash, with and without the plan (“With this plan, cash first goes below zero in November 2026 and is lowest in September 2027, at −$45,128.33. Without it, cash doesn't go below zero in these 12 months.”). Fold the month-by-month table under “Month by month”, as Retirement does.
Expected effect
The question is answered in one sentence; the detail stays one click away.
Acceptance criterion
The summary agrees with the table for every plan in the tests; the plan page on a phone is shorter than today's 4,312 px (measure after); in a walkthrough, members name the first below-zero month (baseline first).
Validation
Automated agreement tests; phone height in the release gates; comprehension question in the walkthrough.

J5 · Am I saving enough for retirement? Moderate occasional · local · confidence medium

User outcome
Understand roughly where current saving leads, and how much the answer depends on the assumptions.
Evidence
E2: “In January 2043, the year you turn 67: $244,195.37”, and each alternative to the cent ($189,232.56, $317,896.20, …), 16 years ahead, from a return the member guessed. The page does say it's in today's dollars and from their assumptions.
Friction / failure
Cent-level figures signal a precision the projection can't have, working against the uncertainty the sensitivity table exists to show. That's a trust risk (principle 14), and the page most likely to be read as advice (GATE-016).
Root cause
Systemic: one money formatter for records and for long-range estimates.
Recommendation
R6. Show projections more than 12 months ahead rounded to the nearest $100 with “about” (“about $244,200”). Keep exact figures for balances, contributions, and anything the member typed.
Expected effect
The estimate reads as an estimate; the alternatives read as a range.
Acceptance criterion
No figure on the retirement page beyond the year-by-year inputs shows cents (automated).
Validation
Test; in the walkthrough, ask members what the figure means and how sure it is (qualitative); the ethics review and the professional check should see it.

J6 · Find what the app can do Moderate common for new members · system-wide · confidence low

User outcome
A member knows the app can project 12 months, compare plans, set goals, project retirement, and bring a record in, when they need it.
Evidence
E2: five features are reached only through a row of small links inside the Coming up card (“The next 60 days · The next 12 months · Plans · Goals · Retirement”) or a sentence in Leaving. For Casey, who has no account, the card opens with “To see a running balance, add your checking account…” and then the same links. Deep pages return only to “Everything”.
Friction / failure
Inferred, not observed: low discoverability of whole features, and a label (Coming up) that doesn't describe what the links lead to.
Root cause
Systemic: the UX contract kept home short by adding links rather than sections, and the entry point was never revisited as features grew from one to six.
Recommendation
R7 (experiment). Move the links out of Coming up into a short “Look ahead” line of their own, directly under Coming up, naming each page by what it answers (“Will we run short? · The next 12 months · What if…? (plans) · Goals · Retirement”). Test against today's layout before committing.
Expected effect
Members find each feature at first try more often.
Acceptance criterion
First-click success for “see what happens if your job stops” and “see if you're saving enough for retirement”, measured before and after (no baseline yet). Home at 50 items stays within 7,812 px on phones.
Validation
A first-click test with the walkthrough participants, or with 5 people from the target group.

J7 · Take my record to a new household Low rare · local · confidence medium

Evidence
E2: save a file, set up a household (a command), open it, choose the file, preview, confirm. Refusals name the fault and place; the same file is refused with its date.
Friction
Setting up a new household needs the command line (mix findependence.setup), which isn't a household task. The preview says “entries” while the rest of the app says items, values, accounts, and debts.
Root cause
Setup was always a developer task in this prototype; “entry” was a new umbrella word.
Recommendation
R8. Replace “entries” with the counted kinds (“4 items and 1 value”), and add “entry” to the glossary's banned synonyms. Leave setup to the pilot's packaging decision, where it belongs.
Acceptance criterion
The glossary test fails on “entry” or “entries” anywhere in the interface; outcome messages name kinds.
Validation
Automated glossary test.

R1a Name what a joint account's view leaves out

Problem solved
J1: a partial running balance on a joint account reads as the account's balance.
Affected users/tasks
Any member viewing Coming up, the next 60 days, or the next 12 months that starts from an account someone else also owns.
Implementation intent
Where the starting accounts include one with other owners, replace the generic note with one that names them: “Mom also owns Joint checking. Items Mom keeps private aren't counted, so the balance after each day is your part of the picture, not the account's balance.” Keep “Below zero” as the fact it is.
Tradeoffs/dependencies
Adds a sentence on home (about one phone line within the remaining margin). It reveals nothing new: co-owners are already shown on the account.
Scope · Reversibility · Confidence
Local · easy · high
Acceptance criteria
With joint checking in the demo, both parents' Coming up names the other parent; a member whose accounts are all their own sees today's note.
Validation
Web tests; glossary and judgment checks; phone budget gate.

R1 Attach items to accounts; count what the viewer can see

Problem solved
J1 and J4: two partial, conflicting projections of the same money.
Affected users/tasks
Couples and anyone sharing an account; the “run short before payday” and “what if” tasks.
Implementation intent
An owner may set “Paid into / from: [account]” on an item they own. An account's running balance applies every item attached to it that the viewer can see (owned or shared), from the latest balance's date. Items not attached keep today's rule (owned only), so Grandma's tuition payments stay out. The note says whether other owners may have attached items the viewer can't see. REQ-138 is revised to say this.
Tradeoffs/dependencies
Changes what REQ-138 counts, and interacts with privacy (PRI-002): nothing private is counted, but sharing a paycheck now has an effect on the other member's view, and members should know that. Needs ACT-001's decision (a ChangeProposal), and a new optional attribute on items. That attribute must be settable after an item is created, which runs into the write-once constraint J3 describes; it could be kept in the member's private record, like links.
Scope · Reversibility · Confidence
Architectural · moderate (the attribute can be ignored if withdrawn) · high that the problem is real; medium that this is the best fix
Acceptance criteria
As in J1: identical balance after each day for both parents when both paychecks are shared and attached; unchanged results for members with no attachments; the tuition case unchanged.
Validation
Core tests; the J1 question in walkthroughs; privacy reviewed in the ethics review.

R2 Prefill a debt's rate and minimum payment

Problem solved
J2: retyping two values that rarely change, with a risk of mistyping them.
Affected users/tasks
Owners updating a debt.
Implementation intent
Fill the rate and minimum fields from the latest balance, keeping them editable; leave the new balance empty; the date stays today.
Tradeoffs/dependencies
A stale rate might be confirmed by inertia. The latest rate and its date are already shown above the form.
Scope · Reversibility · Confidence
Local · easy · high
Acceptance criteria
The debt form shows the latest rate and minimum; saving with only a new balance typed succeeds.
Validation
Web test; walkthrough observation of balance updates.

R3 Update balances on one page

Problem solved
J2: one page visit and one save per account or debt.
Affected users/tasks
Owners of two or more accounts or debts; the weekly check.
Implementation intent
“Update balances” linked from the Balances card: a row per account or debt the member owns, with the latest balance and date for reference and fields as in R2. One save applies every filled row as its own reading. Errors are shown per row with everything typed kept; if any row is invalid, nothing is saved.
Tradeoffs/dependencies
A second place to enter balances (the account page keeps its form). Depends on R2's prefill. On a phone the page is one field per account.
Scope · Reversibility · Confidence
Journey · easy · medium (the weekly frequency is the roadmap's assumption, not observed)
Acceptance criteria
Five balances saved with 1 submit; empty rows skipped; one bad row refuses the whole save and marks the row.
Validation
Web tests; a timed task in walkthroughs, compared with the per-page flow.

R4 Change an amount as a dated, append-only entry

Problem solved
J3: amounts can't be kept current without rebuilding items, and joint items can't be changed at all.
Affected users/tasks
Every member with an amount that changes: rent, pay, premiums, subscriptions.
Implementation intent
A ChangeProposal modelled on CP-013 A: an item's amount and schedule changes are append-only entries with the date they take effect, each sealed like a balance reading. Any owner may add one. Projections use the entry in force on each date; history lists each change.
Tradeoffs/dependencies
New sealed-entry path for the security review (DEF-026); the most expensive change here. Until decided, the item page should explain the current route and what it loses.
Scope · Reversibility · Confidence
Architectural · difficult (stored data) · high
Acceptance criteria
As in J3.
Validation
Core tests, the security review, a walkthrough task.

R5 Summarize a plan's outcome

Problem solved
J4: the answer has to be assembled from 12 rows, mostly below the fold on a phone.
Affected users/tasks
Members comparing plans; plan requests shared with others.
Implementation intent
Compute, with and without the plan, the first month cash goes below zero and the lowest cash and its month; state them in one or two sentences above the table; fold the table under “Month by month” (open by default on desktop is acceptable if testing shows people miss it).
Tradeoffs/dependencies
A summary could be read as a verdict: it must state figures only, no judgment words, which the existing check enforces.
Scope · Reversibility · Confidence
Local · easy · medium
Acceptance criteria
Summary matches the table in tests; the plan page is shorter on a phone than 4,312 px; judgment check passes.
Validation
Automated; comprehension question in walkthroughs.

R6 Round long-range projections

Problem solved
J5: false precision in the estimates most likely to be read as advice.
Affected users/tasks
Retirement page; any future projection beyond 12 months.
Implementation intent
A formatter for estimates: nearest $100 with “about”, used for the retirement balance, the alternatives, and year-by-year balances. Exact formatting stays for inputs and records.
Tradeoffs/dependencies
Year-by-year rows then won't add up to the cent; say “rounded” in the table's note.
Scope · Reversibility · Confidence
System-wide · easy · medium
Acceptance criteria
No cents on estimates on the retirement page; inputs unchanged.
Validation
Test; qualitative question in walkthroughs; the professional check.

R7 A “Look ahead” line (experiment)

Problem solved
J6: whole features are found only through a small link row under an unrelated heading.
Affected users/tasks
New members; anyone looking for plans, goals, or retirement.
Implementation intent
Two variants for a first-click test: (a) today's row; (b) the same links moved out of Coming up into their own line headed “Look ahead”, each named by the question it answers. No new forms on home.
Tradeoffs/dependencies
Uses part of the last 140 px of home's phone budget; must be measured.

System-wide · easy · low (inferred, not observed)

First-click test; phone budget gate.

5 Seamless target state

Dad and Mom open the app on the family laptop on a Sunday evening. Each of them sees the same days of trouble for joint checking, because each attached their paycheck to it and shared it. The page says so, and would say what's missing if they hadn't. Updating balances is one page: five new figures typed, one save, with the rates already filled in. When the mortgage goes up in January, either of them records the new amount from January on, and nothing that referred to it is lost.

Dad opens “What if…?” and reads one sentence: with the plan, cash first goes below zero in November and bottoms out in September. The months are one click away when wanted. On Retirement, Dad reads “about $244,200” and a range of alternatives, and it reads as an estimate. When Alex moves out, the preview names what's coming in the same words the app uses everywhere.

What remains visible on purpose: privacy by default (nothing private is ever counted or shown to others), the statement of assumptions beside every projection, previews before bringing anything in, confirmation before anything irreversible, and the one-member-at-a-time lock on a shared device.

Where complexity stays because the task needs it: deciding what to share with whom; choosing a return and a target for retirement; reading a debt's what-if; and, for a member moving out, setting up a new household.

7 Residual risks and unknowns

  • R1 is a normative decision. Counting a shared paycheck in a spouse's view is useful, but it gives sharing a consequence members may not expect. It belongs to ACT-001 and the ethics review, not to this assessment.
  • R4 needs the security review. It adds a sealed-entry path like readings; building it before DEF-026 is resolved repeats the risk CP-008 A deferred.
