UX-001 · Frontend assessment · 26 September 2026

Findependence Frontend Assessment

Findependence is a local-first household money tool. This assessment covers its browser interface (app/, served on 127.0.0.1), prepared for STUDY-001 with 3–5 households in Sumner, WA. It asks what stands between a household member and the outcomes the tool exists for, and recommends the smallest set of changes that removes it.

Evidence is limited. No target user has used the interface yet. The strongest evidence is the interface itself, measured and screenshotted, plus four problems reported by the project owner (one expert user) while trying it. Confidence ratings below reflect that.

1UX diagnosis

Principal user outcomes

  • Keep an accurate record of money items (rent, wages, a loan) and of what matters to the member, in their own words.
  • Control who in the household sees each thing, with confidence about the current state (the core of OUT-005, member standing).
  • Coordinate on shared things: agree to, or withdraw, changes that need several people.
  • See how money relates to what matters, without being judged (OUT-002, PRI-001).
  • Leave and take one's own record without anyone's help (CAP-002).

Most consequential friction

  1. Everything about every item is on one page, arranged by function rather than by item. With 5 money items and 4 values, the home page is about 5,400 px tall on desktop and carries 46 forms and buttons. "Sharing and ownership" is roughly 60–65% of it. One item's actions are spread across three sections. Three of the owner's four reported problems concern this area.
  2. Amounts can't be entered as money. The amount field accepts only whole numbers, so cents are impossible, and direction (in or out) must be remembered as a minus sign.
  3. Actions offered that can't succeed, and effects that aren't stated. "Stop owning" appears on items where it must fail. "Share" gives the same confirmation whether it applied or is waiting for agreement.
  4. Context is lost after every action and at timeout. Each action reloads the page at the top. An idle lock silently discards the action the member just submitted.

Systemic patterns

  • Function-centred structure. Sections map to system functions (items, values, links, proposals, sharing) rather than to the objects people think about. Scattered actions, duplicated status, and page length all follow from this one choice.
  • Feedback describes the request rather than the result.
  • Vocabulary drift. For example, "item", "money item", "thing", and "something"; "owned by" and "held by"; "share", "let X see", and "also visible to".

Strengths to preserve

  • Plain-language state. Sentences like "Owned by you and Ben. Also visible to Cy", "Waiting for Ben to agree", and "You're the only owner, so changes here take effect right away" (WI-017, WI-019).
  • Names, never internal ids. Formatted amounts; the current owners pre-ticked.
  • Non-evaluative presentation of values. The distribution shows sums and counts with no score (PRI-001).
  • Confirmation only for irreversible actions (delete, leave), and a readable export with a file download.
  • Accessibility foundations. Native controls, a label for every field, visible focus, role="alert" for errors, text contrast of 5.6:1 or better, and a phone layout.
  • Fast and simple. No JavaScript; about 23 ms of server work per page at 200 shared items.

2Journey findings

J1 Control who sees an item High impact

pervasivesystem-wideconfidence: medium–high
User outcome
Know who can see "Rent" right now, and change it, with confidence.
Shortest path
Find Rent, read its status, act on it: one place, one or two actions.
Actual path
The status appears in the items table ("Who else can see it"). Sharing controls are in a separate "Sharing and ownership" section further down, with a block per item, each with an open owner picker. "Stop owning" and "Delete" are back in the items table. Linking is in a third section.
Evidence
E2: page about 5,400 px; the sharing section about 3,500 px for 9 things; 46 forms. E4 (b), (c), (d): all three sharing or proposal confusions.
Friction
Navigation and scanning cost grow with every item; the same status is shown in two places; the member has to remember which section holds which action; risk of acting on the wrong item's block.
Root cause
Systemic: the page is organised by function, and every control for every item is rendered at once.
Recommendation
R1: an item page that holds everything about one item; the home page lists items compactly.
Expected effect
Finding an item becomes a scan of a short list. All of an item's state and actions are in one place.
Acceptance
Every action on an item is reachable from that item's page. The home page holds no per-item sharing or ownership forms. The home page with 10 items is at most two desktop screens tall (a baseline to be measured first).
Validation
A pilot usability task, "Make Groceries visible to Ben, then check who can see Rent", run before and after, comparing completion, errors, time, and page loads.

J2 Record a money item High impact

commonlocalconfidence: high
User outcome
"Groceries, $62.40 spent" is recorded exactly.
Evidence
E5. E1: attributes are {note, amount}; there's no edit operation (ASM-021).
Friction or failure
62.40 can't be entered (the browser rejects it), so the member must round and the record is inaccurate. Direction depends on remembering the sign ("−1200 out, 3000 in" is only a placeholder, gone once typing starts). Any value reaching the server unparsed silently becomes 0. A typo can only be fixed by deleting and re-adding, which loses history, sharing, and links, and is impossible for a jointly owned item.
Root cause
Local: the amount is an integer with the direction encoded in its sign. Architectural: items are immutable.
Recommendation
R2 (entry and validation); R12 (correction, as an experiment).
Acceptance
12.50 and 1,200 are stored exactly. Direction is chosen explicitly. An invalid amount produces a visible message next to the field and nothing is saved. There's no silent coercion (server-side test).
Validation
Unit tests of parsing; a pilot task entering three realistic amounts, comparing entry errors before and after.

occasionaljourneyconfidence: medium

Evidence
E4 (d); E2: "Waiting for you to agree" is the fourth section, below the fold on desktop and far down on a phone.

J4 Change or give up ownership Moderate impact

commonlocalconfidence: high
User outcome
Stop being responsible for something, or hand it to someone.
Evidence
E1: "Stop owning" is shown on every owned item, but a sole owner gets "You're the only owner…" as an error. "Stop owning" on a jointly owned item takes effect at once with no confirmation, and can only be undone if the other owners agree.
Friction
Errors by design: an action is offered that must fail. An irreversible loss of access happens without a checkpoint.
Root cause

Recommendation

J5 Pick up where I left off Moderate impact

pervasive / occasionalsystem-wideconfidence: high
User outcome
After acting, see the result where I was. After a pause, understand why I'm on the unlock screen.
Evidence
E1: every action redirects to /?done=… (the top of the page). The feedback for grant is "Done. If others own it too, they need to agree first." whichever happened. When a session is locked, with_session redirects to /, discarding the submitted form with no message.
Friction
Re-scrolling after every action; uncertainty about the effect; silent loss of input after an idle lock.
Root cause
Systemic: redirects carry no context; feedback is written per action type rather than per outcome; a lock doesn't record why.
Recommendation
R6 (return to context, outcome-specific feedback); R4 (lock notice).
Acceptance
After sharing, the page shows the same item, with "Ben can now see Rent" or "Proposed: waiting for Ben". After an idle timeout, the unlock screen says "Locked after 15 minutes without use. Your last action was not saved."
Validation
Interface tests; a pilot observation of re-scrolling and confusion.

4Recommended changes

Ordered by practical value within each tier. All are compatible with the no-JavaScript constraint.

Necessary corrections

R1 An item page; a compact home list

Change
Add /items/:id, which shows for one thing: amount, owners, who else can see it, pending changes, history, links to values, and every action (share, stop sharing, change owners, link, stop owning or give away, delete). The home page becomes: what's waiting for you; add item and add value; a compact list (name, amount, a visibility summary such as "You and Ben · visible to Cy", and a waiting badge), each entry linking to its page; the distribution summary; leaving. Remove the "Sharing and ownership" section and the per-row buttons from home.
Problem solved
J1 scattering and page length; duplicated status; J6 linking far from the item; inconsistency between items and values.
Affected users and tasks
Every member; every sharing, ownership, linking, and history task.
Implementation intent
New route and view in web.ex and web/html.ex, reusing the existing sharing-block and action renderers. Actions on the item page redirect back to it. No core or vault change.
Trade-offs
One extra page load to reach an item's actions (about 25 ms each). Bulk actions across items get harder, and no current task needs them.
Scope · reversibility · confidence
System-wide · easy (additive routes; the old view can be kept behind a flag) · medium–high
Acceptance criteria
All item actions are available on the item page and none on home, except "add". The home page has no per-item forms. A baseline home-page height for 10 items is measured before, and the after-height is reported against it.
Validation
Interface tests; screenshot heights; pilot tasks T2–T5 (section 6).

R2 Money entry: decimals, explicit direction, visible validation

Change
Replace the amount field with a text input (inputmode="decimal") plus a required "Money in / Money out" choice, defaulting to "Money out". Parse on the server, accepting digits, one decimal point, up to 2 decimal places, and thousands commas. Store integer cents. Reject anything else with a message next to the field, and keep what the member typed. Display with the currency symbol.
Problem solved
J2: cents impossible; sign recall; silent coercion.
Affected users and tasks
Every member, when recording.
Implementation intent
Change to_int/1 to a strict parser that returns an error. Render the form with the error. Existing items store whole units, so either migrate them (×100 on read, marked with unit: :cents) or treat the absence of unit as whole units.
Trade-offs
One extra choice per entry, replacing a remembered convention. The data format changes, so the Vault atom list must include :unit (see the WI-016 lesson).
Scope · reversibility · confidence
Local · moderate (data format) · high
Acceptance criteria
"62.40" with "out" is stored as −6240 cents and shown as "−$62.40". "1,200" is stored as 120000. "12.5.0", "abc", and an empty amount show an error and save nothing. No input path produces a silent 0.
Validation
Parser unit tests, including every case in E5; the pilot entry-error rate.

R3 Offer only possible ownership actions; confirm irreversible ones

Change
A sole owner sees "Give away…", which opens the owner picker with only other people selectable, and never "Stop owning". A joint owner sees "Stop owning…", leading to a confirmation that states the consequence: "You'll stop seeing Rent. Ben keeps it. You can only get it back if Ben agrees."
Problem solved
J4: errors by design; unchecked irreversible loss of access.
Trade-offs
One extra step for joint relinquishment, justified by irreversibility. No confirmation is added anywhere else.
Scope · reversibility · confidence
Local · easy · high
Acceptance criteria
Over all item states in tests, no rendered control can produce sole_owner or not_sole_owner. Joint "Stop owning" requires the confirmation.
Validation
A state-matrix interface test; the pilot error count.

R4 Explain the idle lock

Change
When a request arrives with a session token that has expired, show on the unlock screen: "Locked after 15 minutes without use. Your last action was not saved." Never replay the discarded action, since it would be unsafe after a lock.
Problem solved
J5: silent loss of input; unexplained state change.
Scope · reversibility · confidence
Local · easy · high
Acceptance criteria
A test: expire a session, post an action, then the unlock page shows the message and the vault is unchanged. A first visit shows no such message.
Validation
An interface test.

R5 Visible field boundaries

Change
Change the input and select border from #b8bfcc (1.85:1) to #7b8494 (3.77:1 on white, 3.52:1 on the page background).
Problem solved
WCAG 1.4.11 failure; low-vision users may not find the fields.
Scope · reversibility · confidence
Local · easy · high
Acceptance criteria
Computed contrast of every input and select border is at least 3:1 against its background.
Validation
The contrast script used for E6; an axe check (R10).

High-value improvements

R6 Return to context; outcome-specific feedback

Change
After an action, redirect to the place it came from: the item page with R1, or #item-<id> before R1. Word the message from the actual result: compare the household before and after to choose between "Ben can now see Rent" and "Proposed: waiting for Ben to agree".
Problem solved
J5: re-scrolling and uncertainty.
Trade-offs
Needs the before and after state in the action handler (both are already available in Store.apply).
Scope · reversibility · confidence
System-wide · easy · high
Acceptance criteria
Each of the 12 actions has a test asserting the redirect target and the outcome-specific message.
Validation
Interface tests; pilot observation.

R7 Things waiting for you come first

Change
When something is waiting for this member, render that section first and show "N waiting for you" in the header. When nothing is waiting, keep the explanation, but move it to the item page's ownership area.
Problem solved
J3.
Scope · reversibility · confidence
Local · easy · medium
Acceptance criteria
A waiting item is visible without scrolling at 390 px and 1200 px.
Validation
A screenshot check; pilot task T6.

R8 A leave checklist

Change
"Leave the household…" is always visible. It opens a page listing each owned thing with its required action: "Stop owning" (joint), "Give away or delete" (sole). It also offers "Save your export" first, and ends with the leave confirmation once nothing is owned.
Problem solved
J7: memory and navigation burden at a sensitive moment.
Scope · reversibility · confidence
Journey · easy · medium
Acceptance criteria
A member owning N things can leave from this page alone, in at most N+2 actions (N resolutions, export, confirm).
Validation
Pilot task T7.

R9 One word per concept

Change
Adopt a short glossary and apply it to every string. Proposed terms: item (money), value (what matters to you), owner, can see (grantee), share / stop sharing, give away (transfer), stop owning (relinquish), request (a change waiting for agreement). The same terms go into the study materials.
Problem solved
Recognition cost from synonyms; mismatch with the consent materials.
Scope · reversibility · confidence
System-wide · easy · medium
Acceptance criteria
A test that fails if user-facing strings contain listed non-glossary synonyms.
Validation
A pilot comprehension probe: "What does 'can see' mean here?"

R10 Accessibility verification and table semantics

Change
Add explicit role="table|row|cell" (or use a definition list) where the phone layout sets display:block. Run axe-core against captured pages. Do one keyboard-only walkthrough and one screen-reader walkthrough (NVDA or VoiceOver) of tasks T1–T7.
Problem solved
Unknown assistive-technology operability; a probable loss of semantics on phones.
Scope · reversibility · confidence
System-wide · easy · low (the problem is not yet observed)
Acceptance criteria
No axe violations at "serious" or above. All tasks can be completed by keyboard alone and by screen reader.
Validation
axe report; walkthrough notes.

Experiments

R11 Show money in and money out separately per value

Change
Show two sums per value, one for money in and one for money out, instead of one net total. Still no score or judgement.
Dependency
Confirm this reading of REQ-113, "per-value sums and counts", with the project owner. Validate against STUDY-001 Q3 before building.
Confidence
Low: the problem is inferred, not observed.

R12 Correcting an item

Change
Let owners correct the name or amount of an item.
Dependency
Architectural. Items are immutable (ASM-021), and editing would require key rotation on loss of access plus history of edits. It needs an owner decision and new Requirements.
Before building

  • Give the H1 "Findependence" a visible link style, or none, consistently.

5Seamless target state

A member unlocks the device and sees first anything that needs their answer ("1 waiting for you: Ben invited you to share Holiday"), then a short list of their items and values. Each shows its amount and a one-line summary of who owns it and who else can see it.

To act on something, they open that item. Everything about it is on one page: who can see it, a "Stop sharing" beside each person, a "Share with" offering only people who can't see it yet, and the owners with the current ones ticked. A sentence says whether changes take effect now or wait for someone. Links to their values and the history are there too. After acting, they stay on that page and read what actually happened: "Ben can now see Rent", or "Waiting for Ben to agree".

Recording "$62.40 out" works the first time. If they type something unclear, they're told next to the field and nothing is saved. They never meet a button that can't work. The one action that silently removes their own access asks first.

If they step away and the device locks, the unlock screen says so and says their last action wasn't saved. To leave, one page lists what they still own and what each thing needs, offers their export, and then lets them go.

Complexity that stays, because the task requires it: agreement for jointly owned items and for inviting someone into a value; confirmation before delete, leave, and joint "Stop owning"; the statement of what isn't encrypted; the unrecoverable passphrase; one person at a time on the device.

6Verification plan

Establish the baseline first

Run the pilot tasks below on the current interface before changing anything. Every "reduce" or "fewer" target above is judged against that baseline. No numeric targets are set until then.

Pilot usability test

3–5 adults who are not STUDY-001 participants, using a demo household of made-up data, moderated, 30–40 minutes each, run before and after the changes. It uses no real financial data. Confirm with the ethics body whether it counts as research.

Automated and technical checks

7Residual risks and unknowns

  • The evidence is thin. One expert user and screenshots of demo data. Real households may struggle with different things, such as the concept of consent-based sharing, or passphrases. The pilot is the cheapest way to reduce this.
  • Security design may change the interface. If the owner adopts signed changes (DEF-028, option a), agreeing becomes a cryptographic act, and wording and flows around requests may need revisiting.
  • Immutability. Without R12, mistakes stay costly. It's an architectural decision, not a UI one.
  • The no-JavaScript constraint. Rules out inline editing and live updates. Full page loads are fast, but each one resets focus and, without R6, scroll position.
  • Coercion and the shared device (DEF-016). No interface change can protect a member from being watched or pressured at the screen. The auto-lock and "Lock" button are the only controls.
  • Setup is run by the researcher, and membership is fixed (ASM-022). Households can't add a member later. Out of scope here, but visible to participants.
  • The assessor built the interface. This assessment was produced by the same agent that built the interface, so an independent heuristic review would reduce the bias.
