Findependence Visual Language Review

This review looks at the interface's visual language as a whole system: the rules that decide how every page looks, in every state, theme, and width. It doesn't judge whether the product is attractive. UX-003 fixed the interface's craft (focus, alignment, control heights, links, errors, the type scale), and those fixes still hold: geometry.py finds 0 failures on 70 pages at 1200 and 390 px. So this review covers what UX-003 left out or couldn't see: forced colours and dark preference, button emphasis across the whole product, the grammar of numbers, the upper type scale, reading measure, the phone row grids, and the tokens still missing.

Prepared by ACT-002, the same agent that built this interface, so read it with that bias in mind. It is a proposal, not a canonical change: it changes no code, Requirement, or ratified artifact. Every figure comes from the demo family, whose names, figures, and relationships are invented. No brand guidance, design files, or stated visual intent exist, so the review treats the observed restraint as intended (section 10 lists this as an open decision).

1Visual system diagnosis

The current visual language

One inline stylesheet (web.ex @css, lines 1144–1251) with no JavaScript, web fonts, images, icons, shadows, or motion. Every page is a column of white cards (radius 10 px, 1 px border) on a pale grey ground, 56 rem wide, set in the platform's system-ui at 16 px / 1.5. Since UX-003 the stylesheet is tokenized: 16 colour tokens, 6 type sizes, 2 control heights, 4 radii, and a column width. The only literal colour left outside :root is #fff (3 uses). Semantic colour is used strictly by meaning: blue for interactive, red for error and loss, green for success, amber for "needs you", and blue tint for information.

Dominant principles (inferred, and consistent across the product)

  • Words carry state; colour reinforces it. "Below zero", "Waiting for Ben to agree", "That wasn't saved": every state is written, and colour is added on top. This is the system's strongest property, and it is why forced colours degrade the appearance but not the meaning (V1).
  • Nothing moves. A recorded rule (the @css comment, tested in ux003_test.exs): every change is a new page that says what happened.
  • Money is typeset with care. Tabular figures, a true minus sign, an explicit plus for money in, and headers aligned over their figures.
  • Restraint as non-paternalism. No visual device ranks, scores, or celebrates. This follows from PRI-001 and the "nothing is scored" rule in the product's wording.

Strongest qualities to preserve

Semantic honesty of colour; text-first state; zero layout shift (first paint is final paint); the phone table grids; the money typesetting; one frame (header, cards, and footer share the 56 rem column). All of UX-003's geometry criteria still pass.

Most significant deficiencies

  1. Forced colours erase five state treatments (V1). Under Windows High Contrast (emulated in Chromium), an invalid field looks identical to a valid one; result messages lose their box; "Below zero" and the waiting badge lose their pill; and the attention card looks like every other card. The words survive, but the visual cues don't.
  2. The "needs you" card is marked by a 2.6:1 border (V2), under the 3:1 non-text guideline, and 1 px wide. It is the only card whose border means something.
  3. Button emphasis encodes "this submits a form", not priority (V3). The leave checklist has 7 solid primary buttons in one card; the plan, card-debt, and 401(k) pages have 3 in one card. Row actions elsewhere (Agree, Withdraw, Remove, Stop owning) use the compact outline style, so the same role gets two treatments.
  4. "About" means two different things (V4). On retirement it means rounded to $100 with no cents ("about $244,200"). On 11 other captured states it comes before an exact-looking figure ("about $4,291.12 of interest", "About +$5,741.67 a month"). The money typesetting, the product's one signature, is inconsistent in how it expresses precision.

Theme, responsive, and implementation summary

Themes: light only. Dark preference renders byte-identical to light (verified by image hash), which is consistent, not broken. Forced colours is the one alternative appearance that actually occurs, and it is the weak one (V1). Responsive: composed, not squeezed. The weak point is the phone item rows, whose second-line columns start at 5 different x positions (V8). Implementation drift: small. The page-title step depends on a structural selector, so pages without a back link have no title step (V5). Spacing has no tokens (V10).

3Findings

Accessibility-critical

V1 Forced colours erase five state treatments high

Themingpervasiveproduct-widesmalleasyconfidence high
Evidence
E3, home and field-error pages under emulated forced-colors: active: the attention card border computes to the same colour and width as every card (rgb(0,0,0), 1 px); [aria-invalid=true] computes to the same border as valid inputs (the 1 px box-shadow is dropped); .msg has no border and its tint is removed; .below and .badge have no border and lose their fill; solid and outline buttons compute identically.
Finding
Every state that is marked by fill or box-shadow alone disappears. The words remain, so meaning survives, but a person who uses high contrast because they have low vision loses the visual cues that everyone else gets: which field is wrong, where the result message starts and ends, which card needs them.
Secondary
State expression, Surface
Root cause
States are expressed through background and box-shadow, which forced colours replace or remove; borders and outlines, which survive, are not used for those states. There is no @media (forced-colors: active) rule.
Recommendation
S1. One forced-colours block that gives each state a border, the one property forced colours keeps.
Acceptance
Under forced colours: an invalid control's border is at least 2 px thicker than a valid one's (or uses a distinct system colour); each .msg, .below, and .badge has a visible 1 px border; the attention card's border is at least 3 px; the primary button differs from the outline button (for example ButtonText border 2 px).
Validation
A new mode in the geometry or capture tooling: re-run the E3 computed-style check with emulation on and assert each difference; a human check in real Windows High Contrast (smallest additional evidence).

V2 The "needs you" card is marked by a 2.6:1, 1 px border moderate

State expressionrepeatedcomponentsmalleasyconfidence high
Evidence
E5: --attention #c79a1e on white is 2.6:1; the other cards' border --line is 1.36:1. E2: on Dad's home page the difference is a slightly warmer hairline.
Finding
The one card that asks for the person's decision is marked by the weakest cue on the page. The heading "Waiting for you" says it, so WCAG 1.4.11 is arguably not engaged, but the visual cue as designed is below the non-text guideline and easy to miss when scanning.
Root cause
The border colour was chosen to harmonize with the badge fill, not for contrast.
Recommendation
S2. An attention border of at least 3:1 and heavier than the other cards' (for example a 4 px inline-start edge in a darker amber such as --attention-ink, 7:1 range), keeping the 1 px outline elsewhere.
Acceptance
The attention indicator is ≥ 3:1 against the card and page ground, and ≥ 2 px wider than ordinary card borders; the page's height doesn't change.
Validation
Contrast computed from tokens (extend the token audit); screenshot of Dad's home at both widths.

Structural corrections

V3 Solid emphasis follows form mechanics, not priority moderate

Visual hierarchyrepeatedpatternsmalleasyconfidence high
Evidence
E4: solid buttons per card, highest on each page: leave 7 ("Do this" on each owned thing), plan / Visa / HELOC / 401(k) / IRA / checking 3, item pages 2. E1: button defaults to solid; outline comes only from context selectors (.inline button, td button). Per-row actions in lists (Agree, Withdraw, Remove, Stop owning) are compact outline; the leave checklist's per-row "Do this" is solid.
Finding
Two treatments for one role (an action on one row of a list), and a card where seven equal primaries compete, so none leads. On the leave page the "Stop owning" and "Withdraw" rows, which are outline, look less important than seven solid rows, which is backwards.
Secondary
Aesthetic coherence
Root cause
Emphasis is inferred from where the button sits in the markup (a form.row submit is solid) instead of chosen by role. UX-003 C7 added a variant argument, but only for danger and compact; the default remains solid.
Recommendation
S1b (rule). Record the emphasis rule: at most one solid button per card, for the action the card exists for; an action repeated per row uses the compact outline variant, wherever the row is. Apply it to the leave checklist now. The multi-form plan card stays under UX-003 X3 as an experiment.
Tradeoffs
Outline buttons are less conspicuous to people unsure what to press. The leave page's step numbers and headings still lead them.
Acceptance
On the 75 captured states, no card has more than one solid button except the plan's "Add a step" card (listed as the X3 exception); every per-row action in a list is compact outline.
Validation
The E4 counter as a test over captured pages; screenshot of the leave page.

V4 "About" means rounded in one place and precise-looking in eleven moderate

Typography (numeric)Brand expressionrepeatedsubsystemsmallmoderateconfidence high
Evidence
E4: 11 of 75 states show "about" before a figure with cents: debt interest ("about $129.12", "about $4,291.12 of interest"), setting aside ("about $390.00 a month"), item monthly equivalents ("About +$5,741.67 a month"). Retirement shows "about $244,200", and its note says estimates are rounded to $100. E1: about/1 rounds; the other sites write the word "about" before plain_amount/1. E7: UX-003 K10's acceptance says "No 'about' figure shows cents"; WI-045 marked it met, but only retirement was changed, and balances_web_test.exs:141 and cash_flow_web_test.exs:145 pin cents after "about".
Finding
The product's most characteristic visual trait, honest and exact money, uses one word with two precision grammars. "About $4,291.12" signals both approximation and cent-level precision.
Root cause
No rule for how an estimate is written; "about" is prose, not a formatter.
Recommendation
S3. A decision (D3) by ACT-001, because it changes wording that tests and possibly Requirements pin: either (a) every "about" figure goes through one formatter that rounds to a stated unit (whole dollars for monthly amounts and interest, $100 for projections), or (b) "about" is removed where the figure is exact arithmetic and kept only for rounded ones.
Addendum (REV-060): REQ-135 and REQ-145 say a debt's monthly interest and payoff interest are shown "as a fact" (and "as facts at its latest reading"). So option (a) would move those figures further from their Requirements, and the current "about $129.12" already sits against them. The recommendation that fits the Requirements is (b) throughout: no "about" on debt interest, payoff interest, setting aside (REQ-140), or monthly averages, which keep their cents; "about" with rounding only on the retirement projections (REQ-151, REQ-153).
Acceptance
Every figure after "about" is produced by the estimate formatter (unit test on the helper); no captured state shows "about" followed by cents.
Validation
The E4 regular expression as a test over captured pages.

V5 The top of the type scale doesn't separate roles, and the title step depends on a back link low–moderate

TypographyImplementation fidelitypervasivesystemsmalleasyconfidence medium
Evidence
E4: rendered sizes 12.8 / 14 / 16 / 18.4 / 20 / 22.4 px; weights 400, 600, 700. The wordmark and .amount-big are both 20 px, above every h2 (18.4 px). The title rule is .back~section:first-of-type>h2: confirm pages ("Delete the plan…?") and home have no title step. In DejaVu, 600 and 700 render the same.
Finding
Three steps within 22% of each other carry four roles (section heading, product name, hero figure, page title). The product name outranks the page's own sections, and pages without a back link have no title.
Root cause
C11 named the existing sizes rather than re-deriving them; the page-title role was attached by selector because the page helper has no title slot.
Recommendation
S4. A .page-title class emitted explicitly by the renderers on each page's first heading (visual only; the h1 question stays with REV-049). Display steps with a ratio of at least 1.2: section 1.125 rem, title 1.5 rem; wordmark at the section step; hero amount at the section step, weight 400. Two weights: 400 and 700.
Acceptance
Every page has exactly one .page-title; adjacent display steps differ by ≥ 1.2×; the wordmark is not larger than a section heading; only weights 400 and 700 appear in @css.
Validation
Token audit; the E4 counter; screenshots of home, an item, and a confirm page.

V8 Phone item rows have no shared column edges low–moderate

Responsive compositionpervasive on phonescomponentsmalleasyconfidence high
Evidence
E2/E4, Dad's home at 390 px: td.vis starts at 130, 134, 142, 195, or 199 px across 18 rows; the amount cell wraps in 4 of 18 rows, breaking inside the frequency ("twice a / year", "every two / weeks", "a year, / irregular"). The next 60 days: the "Below zero" pill sits on its own line at the row's right edge, beneath rather than beside the balance it qualifies.

Acceptance

Validation
A geometry check at 390 and 320 px; phone budget re-measured.

Systemic refinements and polish

V9 No hover or pressed state on any styled button low

State expressionpervasivesystemsmalleasyconfidence high
Evidence
E1: zero :hover or :active rules. Once a button has a background, Chromium drops its native press styling.
Finding
With a mouse, nothing acknowledges pointing at or pressing a button until the next page arrives. Server times under 25 ms keep this minor.
Recommendation
S8. --accent-strong for solid hover and press; --info-bg fill for outline hover. No transition (the no-motion rule holds).
Acceptance
Every button variant has a distinct hover and active computed background; no transition in @css.
Validation
Computed style under DevTools forcePseudoState.

7Systemic findings

  • Colour-only-by-fill states (tokens and theming): V1 and V2 share a cause. States are drawn with background and hairlines; borders and weight would survive both forced colours and scanning.
  • Emphasis inferred from markup context (component architecture): V3, and UX-003 X3.
  • Prose where a formatter is needed (content-system boundary): V4.
  • Named rather than designed tokens (type and spacing): V5 and V10. UX-003 turned literals into names; the steps themselves weren't re-derived.
  • Per-row grids with no shared tracks (responsive architecture): V8.
  • Isolated symptoms, not systemic: V6 (one page family), P1, P2.

9Target visual language

  • Composition: one column, one card per concern, each with one h2; the page's name at the top as its largest text.
  • Typography: body 1 rem / 1.5; display steps at ratios of 1.2 or more; weights 400 and 700 only; explanatory prose ≤ 72ch; every estimate written by one formatter, so "about" always means rounded.
  • Spacing: five named steps; exceptions listed by name.
  • Colour: unchanged meanings; every state indicator ≥ 3:1, including attention.
  • Form and surface: flat, border-defined cards and pills; three radii; no shadows (recorded as a rule).
  • Icons, imagery, motion: none; the no-motion rule holds for hover and press states too.
  • Hierarchy: at most one solid button per card; row actions compact outline; red only for loss.
  • States: written first; each drawn with a border or weight that survives forced colours; hover and press visible.
  • Brand: expressed through restraint and exact money, unless ACT-001 decides otherwise (D1).
  • Themes: light, plus forced colours as a supported mode with its own rule block; dark only by decision (D2), and then as a token swap.
  • Responsive: phone rows share tracks; phrases don't break inside themselves; markers sit beside what they qualify.
  • Fidelity: no literal outside :root; every rule above checked by geometry.py, a token audit, or a markup test.

11Residual risks and unknowns

  • No stated visual or brand intent (D1). Restraint is treated as intended. Smallest step: ACT-001 says whether a recognizable identity (wordmark, accent, typeface) is wanted before the pilot, or whether restraint is the identity.
  • Dark appearance (D2). No evidence either way that households want it. Smallest step: note requests in WALKTHROUGH-001; S9 keeps the option cheap.
  • Precision wording (D3). Pinned by tests, and REQ-135 and REQ-145 call the debt figures facts (see V4's addendum). Smallest step: ACT-001 confirms (b).
  • One engine, one font. DejaVu has no 600 weight and is wider than platform fonts; wrapping (V8) and weight (V5) will differ. Smallest step: one Safari and one Windows pass.
  • Forced colours by emulation. Real Windows High Contrast themes vary. Smallest step: one pass on Windows.
  • Aesthetic subjectivity. V6, V7, and X4 rest on established guidance plus expert judgment, not user evidence.
  • Not assessed: print, 400% zoom beyond the 320 px reflow check, screen-reader rendering of any change, and localization (the product is English only).
