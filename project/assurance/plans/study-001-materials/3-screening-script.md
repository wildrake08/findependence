# Private safety screening: researcher script

**DRAFT for ethics review. For the researcher only; not handed to participants.**

## Why screen at all

The tool is built to protect each person's information, but it is an early prototype. It is not a security product (DEF-026, ASM-013). If someone in a household is being controlled or is afraid of another member, a study about money and privacy could make things worse for them. This first study therefore does not include households where anyone reports that (STUDY-001 §4). That is a choice to protect people, not a judgement about them.

## Rules

1. **Screen each adult alone,** where no other household member can see or hear, including through a door or on the phone. If privacy can't be ensured, reschedule, with a neutral reason ("let's find a better time").
2. **Ask every adult the same questions,** in the same way, whatever you expect the answers to be.
3. **Give every adult the safety-resources sheet,** whatever they answer. It is part of the standard information pack, so receiving it means nothing.
4. **Write down only "eligible" or "not eligible" for the household.** Never record who answered what, or why.
5. **If a household is not eligible, tell every adult the same thing,** in the same words (see below), so nobody can work out whose answer caused it.
6. **Don't counsel, advise, or investigate.** You are not a support worker. Offer the resources, and follow the ethics body's instructions for [ANY DISCLOSURE PROTOCOL / REPORTING DUTIES].

## Script

> "Before we go further, I ask everyone the same few questions, privately. They help make sure taking part is safe and comfortable for you. You don't have to answer any of them, and nothing you say here will be shared with anyone in your household."

1. "Is there anyone in your household who controls your money in a way that worries you? For example, taking your money, stopping you from getting money, or making all the decisions without you."
2. "Would anything bad happen to you if someone in your household found out what you share with me, or what you put into the tool?"
3. "Do you ever feel afraid of anyone in your household?"
4. "Is there anything else that would make it hard or unsafe for you to take part?"

**If the answer to every question is "no",** or the person chooses not to answer:
> "Thank you. Here's the information pack. It includes some contacts everyone gets, just in case they're ever useful."

**If any answer is "yes", or suggests any concern:** don't probe for details.
> "Thank you for telling me. That matters. This version of the study isn't the right fit, and I'd rather not take any chance of making things harder for you. Here's the information pack. It has contacts that can help, any time, confidentially. You don't need to explain anything to anyone about today."

Then follow [DISCLOSURE PROTOCOL SET BY ETHICS BODY]. If someone is in immediate danger, call 911.

## Telling the household

To **every** adult, individually, in the same words:
> "Thank you for your interest. We're not able to include your household in this round of the study. That's based on how we've set up this early version, not on anything anyone did wrong. Everyone gets this same message."

Record only: `household [CODE] — not eligible — [DATE]`.
