## What is this?

We are testing an early version of a tool called **Findependence**. It helps people in a household keep track of money, and decide for themselves who in the household sees what.

We want to learn whether it is useful, whether it is easy to use, and what gets in the way. **We are testing the tool, not you.** There are no right or wrong answers.

## What would I do?

- **4 weeks** of using the tool whenever you like, on [a device you provide / a laptop we lend you].
- **Three private conversations** with the researcher, about [30–45] minutes each: at the start, around week 2, and at the end.
- **An optional group conversation** at the end, only if every adult in your household wants one.

Each adult in your household takes part separately. Your conversations with the researcher are **just between you and the researcher**.

## What does the tool do?

- You add money items, such as rent, wages, or a loan, and things that matter to you, in your own words.
- **You decide who in your household can see each thing you add.** Nobody sees it unless you choose to share it.
- Some things can be shared by two or more people. Shared things need everyone involved to agree before changes happen, but **anyone can stop sharing, or step away from something shared, on their own.**
- You can see how your money lines up with what matters to you. **The tool never scores, judges, or advises.**
- You can **take your information with you and leave** at any time, without anyone else's help.

## What stays private, and what doesn't

Please read this part carefully.

- **Everything stays on the device.** Nothing is sent over the internet, and the researcher does not get a copy of your information.
- **What you write is locked with your passphrase.** Descriptions, amounts, and your values can only be opened by the people you've allowed to see them. Your links between money and values can only ever be opened by you.
- **Some things are not locked.** Someone with access to the device, and the know-how to look inside its files, could see:
  - the names of the people in the household;
  - how many things each person has added;
  - who owns each thing, and who it is shared with;
  - proposals waiting for agreement, such as a proposal to share something with a particular person.

  They could **not** see what those things are or how much they are for.
- **If you forget your passphrase, it can't be recovered.** Nobody, including the researcher, can reset it.
- **Only one person uses the tool at a time**, and it locks itself after 15 minutes. Please press **Lock** when you're done.

## What does the researcher write down?

- Notes on what you tell us, in your own words.
- Where the tool was confusing or got in the way.
- How long some simple tasks take.

**We never write down** amounts, account numbers, passwords, what your items or values are, or any health details. We never take screenshots or copies of the tool.

We only record audio if **everyone present** agrees at the start of that conversation (Washington law requires this). You can say no, and still take part.

## Could this be uncomfortable?

It might be. Talking about money can bring up disagreements, or things people would rather keep private. You can skip any question, take a break, or stop at any time. You never have to explain why.

## Stopping

**You can stop at any time, for any reason, without telling anyone why.**

- Tell the researcher, [CONTACT].
- We won't tell the other people in your household why you stopped, or that it was your decision.
- If you want, we will destroy our notes from your conversations.
- The other adults in your household can carry on without you if they want to.

Before you stop, you can save your own information from the tool (**See everything you'd take with you → Save as a file**), and then delete it from the device.
